# CHECKPOINT 1G — DO TUSCAN PROVINCES AGREE? (the outcome's internal consistency test)

The single most informative test available on this dataset, and the one Veneto could never
run: **nine independently-monitored provinces, do they agree on which seasons were bad?**

Panel composition, observer identity and visit intensity are all *province-specific*. They
cannot manufacture agreement between separate provincial networks. Season weather can.

## Result — every single pair agrees

| | bunches (var 36) | leaves (var 34) |
|---|---|---|
| provinces with ≥10 seasons | 9 | 9 |
| pairs tested | **36** | **36** |
| pairs positive | **36 / 36** | **36 / 36** |
| mean ρ | **+0.622** | **+0.631** |
| range | +0.317 … +0.953 | +0.382 … +0.841 |
| permutation p (years shuffled within province, 3 000 draws) | **0.00033** | **0.00033** |

Strongest pairs are geographically coherent: Livorno–Pistoia +0.953, Pisa–Pistoia +0.935,
Firenze–Siena +0.892 (the Chianti axis). Weakest are the most climatically distinct:
Arezzo–Grosseto +0.317 (inland Apennine valley vs coastal Maremma), Massa-Carrara–Pisa
+0.390.

Even the weakest pair is positive. There is no province that disagrees with the rest.

## Compared with the Andalucía benchmark

| | Andalucía (RAIF) | **Toscana** |
|---|---|---|
| pairs | 6 | **36** |
| all positive | yes | **yes** |
| mean ρ | +0.490 | **+0.622** |
| permutation p | 0.0004 | **0.00033** |

Toscana is the stronger of the two on every axis: six times the pairs, higher mean
agreement, same significance.

## What this establishes, and what it does not

**Establishes.** There is a genuine *regional season effect* in Tuscan vine peronospora.
The outcome is not an artefact of who observed, of how many sites were in the panel, or of
visit intensity — the earlier tests showed ρ(n_orgs, outcome) = −0.011 and
ρ(n_sites, outcome) = +0.071, and this one shows nine separate networks tracking the same
seasons together.

**Does not establish.** That the season effect is *predictable in advance*. Province
agreement says the signal is real; the horizon curve says when it becomes legible, and it
says nothing before 31 May clears correction. A real signal and a forecastable signal are
different claims, and only the first is proved here.
